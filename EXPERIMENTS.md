# Technical Experiments

This document records practical technical experiments conducted during my development as a software engineer.

The goal is to go beyond learning concepts theoretically: formulate questions, test ideas, analyze results, and document what I discover.

## Purpose

I use experiments to develop problem-solving skills, understand how software works internally, and learn to evaluate technical decisions based on evidence rather than assumptions.

Experiments may cover:

- Python and programming fundamentals
- Algorithms and data structures
- Computer science concepts
- Data analysis and Machine Learning
- Artificial Intelligence systems
- Software architecture and engineering practices

## Experiment Structure

Each experiment should follow a consistent structure.

### 1. Research Question

What exactly am I trying to understand or find out?

### 2. Hypothesis

What do I expect to happen, and why?

A hypothesis is an assumption to be tested, not a confirmed fact.

### 3. Method

How will I test the hypothesis?

This section may include code, tools, datasets, configurations, and experimental conditions.

### 4. Results

What actually happened?

Results should describe observable outcomes rather than expectations.

### 5. Analysis

Why did I get these results?

Were there unexpected outcomes, limitations, errors, or factors that could have influenced the experiment?

### 6. Conclusion

What did I learn?

Was the original hypothesis supported, rejected, or left inconclusive?

### 7. Next Steps

What could I investigate or improve in a future experiment?

## Principles

- Test assumptions instead of treating them as facts.
- Document unsuccessful experiments as well as successful ones.
- Prefer reproducible results over unsupported claims.
- Explain technical decisions and their trade-offs.
- Be honest about limitations and uncertainty.
- Keep experiments small enough to understand and evaluate.

## Experiment Log

Experiments will be added here as they are conducted and documented.

Each experiment should contain enough information for another person to understand what was tested, how it was tested, and what conclusions can reasonably be drawn.
